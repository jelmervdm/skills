# Rowing Readiness, Injury Prevention & Recovery

Protocol for protecting the lumbar spine, preventing rib stress fractures, managing forearm/grip strain, and monitoring recovery markers.

---

## 1. Lumbar Spine Protection & Core Mobility

The lumbar spine undergoes high compressive loads during the catch phase, especially when combined with spinal flexion.

### Lumbar Safety Guidelines

- **Hinge at Hips**: Rotate torso forward from the hip joints at the catch; avoid flexing the lumbar spine.
- **Limit High Drag Factors**: Keep drag factor $\le 130$ for endurance rowing to prevent excessive spinal shear force.
- **Post-Workout Decompression**: Perform 5–10 minutes of cat-cow movements, cobra stretch, and child's pose following long ergometer sessions.
- **Core Stability**: Maintain regular anti-rotation and bracing exercises (Dead Bug, Pallof Press, Planks) $3\times$ weekly.

---

## 2. Rib Stress Fracture Avoidance

Rib stress fractures (typically ribs 4–9) occur from repetitive pulling of the serratus anterior and intercostal muscles under high training volume.

### Warning Signs & Prevention

- **Early Warning**: Point tenderness on the lateral or posterior ribcage, or pain when taking deep breaths or coughing.
- **Action**: Immediately reduce handle drag, suspend high-power strokes, and cross-train on stationary bike/swimming until evaluated.
- **Prevention**: Avoid sudden spikes in weekly training meters ($>15\%$ increase per week) and avoid heavy ergometer rowing with fatigued serratus muscles.

---

## 3. Forearm & Grip Fatigue Management

- **Loose Grip**: Hold handle loosely in fingers at catch; do not squeeze or white-knuckle the handle.
- **Blister Care**: Keep hands clean, apply friction tape or hydrocolloid bandages on open blisters, and avoid popping blisters before training.
