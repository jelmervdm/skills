# Rowing Workout Analysis & Stroke Mechanics

Diagnostic framework for evaluating rowing stroke force profiles, drive-to-recovery rhythm, split decay, and heart rate decoupling.

---

## 1. Force Curve & Drive Mechanics

The Concept2 monitor force curve represents instantaneous force applied throughout the drive phase.

### Ideal Force Curve Characteristics

- **Smooth Parabolic Arch**: Smooth rise to peak force during mid-leg drive, transitioning seamlessly through trunk back-swing into arm draw.
- **Early Peak Force**: Peak force occurs within the first $35–45\%$ of the drive length (driven by explosive leg extension).

### Common Force Curve Faults

- **Double Peak ("Shooting the Slides")**: Legs extend rapidly without moving the handle, followed by a late upper-body jerk. Result: Spinal load spike and loss of power.
- **Flat Top / Lagging Catch**: Slow force development at the catch due to loose grip, bent arms, or delayed engagement.

---

## 2. Drive-to-Recovery Ratio & Rhythm

Optimal stroke rhythm balances high impulse on the drive with relaxation on the recovery:

$$\text{Rhythm Ratio} = \frac{T_{\text{recovery}}}{T_{\text{drive}}} \approx 2.0$$

- **Drive Phase ($T_{\text{drive}}$)**: Fast, explosive leg drive ($0.7 - 0.9\text{ seconds}$).
- **Recovery Phase ($T_{\text{recovery}}$)**: Controlled slide forward ($1.4 - 1.8\text{ seconds}$).
- **Slide Rushing**: Rushing forward into the catch ($T_{\text{recovery}} \le T_{\text{drive}}$) causes check on the shell (on water) or excessive hip flexor strain (on ergometer).

---

## 3. Pacing Decay & Aerobic Decoupling

Evaluating fatigue and energy system contribution across interval sets:

$$\text{Aerobic Decoupling (\%)} = \left( \frac{\text{HR}_{\text{half2}} / \text{Watts}_{\text{half2}}}{\text{HR}_{\text{half1}} / \text{Watts}_{\text{half1}}} - 1 \right) \times 100$$

- **Target Decoupling**: $\le 5\%$ for steady-state UT2 sessions.
- **Decoupling $> 5\%$**: Indicates glycogen depletion, overheating, or insufficient aerobic base.
