# Running Workout Analysis & Biomechanical Evaluation

Structured protocol for analyzing completed running workouts, evaluating pace decoupling, stride metrics, and ground contact dynamics.

---

## 1. Biomechanical Metrics & Benchmarks

| Metric | Target Range | Physiological Significance |
|--------|--------------|----------------------------|
| **Cadence (Stride Rate)** | $170 - 185\text{ spm}$ | Lower cadence ($< 160\text{ spm}$) increases stride length, overstriding, and joint peak shock load. |
| **Ground Contact Time (GCT)** | $200 - 240\text{ ms}$ | Shorter GCT indicates better leg stiffness and elastic energy return. |
| **GCT Balance** | $50.0\% / 50.0\%$ | Asymmetry ($> 51.5\% / 48.5\%$) indicates muscle imbalance or compensation for fatigue/injury. |
| **Vertical Oscillation** | $6.0 - 9.0\text{ cm}$ | Excessive vertical bounce ($> 10\text{ cm}$) wastes kinetic energy vertically instead of horizontally. |

---

## 2. Pace Decoupling (Pace:HR Drift)

Measures cardiovascular drift relative to running speed during steady-state runs:

$$\text{Efficiency Factor (EF)} = \frac{\text{Speed (m/min)}}{\text{Average Heart Rate (bpm)}}$$

$$\text{Decoupling (\%)} = \left(1 - \frac{\text{EF}_{\text{Second Half}}}{\text{EF}_{\text{First Half}}}\right) \times 100$$

- **$< 5\%$**: Aerobically stable. Target long run pace is appropriate.
- **$> 5\%$**: Excessive drift. Indicates poor endurance, heat strain, or inadequate hydration.
